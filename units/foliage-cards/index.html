    Foliage Cards, Overdraw and Atlases

    Why foliage is alpha-tested and why its overdraw costs more, tight cutouts against wasted pixels, depth prepasses, shadow casters, one atlas per species against shared atlases and texture arrays, and mipmaps for foliage atlases, built in Unity HDRP and Unreal.

    Builds on Transparency and Alpha and Frame Profiling. Build chapters for Unity HDRP and Unreal Engine.

    Alpha test, not blending

    Foliage in games is almost always opaque with an alpha test (alpha clip, masked): each pixel is either fully leaf or discarded. Alpha blending would need sorting thousands of intersecting cards back to front, which is impossible per pixel, and blended surfaces do not write depth, so shadows, ambient occlusion, depth of field, fog and temporal effects would all ignore them. Blending is reserved for special cases: soft grass tips in a cinematic, a few hero plants.

    Explained once, used here

    Alpha test: clip(alpha − cutoff) gives opaque, sortable cutouts; mip averaging thins them in the distance unless coverage is preserved in the mips.

    Open the full explanation

    Two related tools from the same unit: alpha to coverage softens cut edges with MSAA (rare in deferred renderers), and dithered transparency, which TAA turns into smooth fades, is how foliage cross-fades between LODs.

    Why foliage overdraw is expensive

    Overdraw costs every renderer (overdraw), but alpha-tested foliage has an extra problem. For opaque geometry the GPU can test depth before the pixel shader and skip hidden pixels. A shader that may discard a pixel can only write depth after it has run, so the GPU cannot reject later layers until the earlier ones have finished, and many GPUs then run the shader for most of the layers. A crown is many layers of cards, each mostly transparent:

    Three levers: fewer layers (fewer, better-placed cards; LODs that merge them), less transparent area per card (tight cutouts), and making sure only one layer is fully shaded (depth prepass). Sorting instances roughly front to back helps too, where the engine allows it.

    Tight cutouts: vertices against transparent pixels

    A leaf cluster on a quad leaves the corners transparent, yet every pixel of the quad runs at least the alpha test. Cutting the card along the cluster's outline with a few more vertices removes most of that waste. Vertices are processed once per card per pass, pixels many times, so up to a point the trade is very favourable:

    Card generators in SpeedTree and the Unreal and Unity community tools fit outlines automatically, typically with 6–8 vertices. Do not go further than the screen size justifies: below about ten pixels per triangle, triangle rasterisation gets inefficient (quads and tiny triangles), so the distant LODs go back to simple quads or to an impostor.

    Depth prepass

    A depth prepass draws the foliage twice: first with a shader that only reads the alpha texture, clips and writes depth; then with the full shader and the depth test set to equal, so only the one visible fragment per pixel is shaded, and that pass needs no clip. The price is a second geometry pass, including the wind in the vertex shader:

    Deferred renderers usually already have one: HDRP renders alpha-tested opaque materials in its depth prepass, and Unreal's early depth pass can include masked meshes (Early Z-pass = Opaque and masked meshes). Forward renderers often make it a choice per material. Whether it pays depends on how many layers get shaded and how heavy the vertex shader is; profile both.

    Shadow casters

    Every foliage instance in a shadow cascade is drawn again into the shadow map, with its wind and its alpha test. Shadow passes are often a larger share of foliage cost than the main view:

    • No shadows from grass and small plants; let a contact-shadow or AO term darken their base.
    • A simpler LOD or a dedicated shadow proxy for shadow casting (lower LOD bias in shadow passes, or a separate shadow-only mesh).
    • Shorter shadow distances for foliage than for buildings; distant trees can cast from an impostor or not at all.
    • Cached or static shadow cascades for vegetation that only sways slightly (HDRP and Unreal both offer cached shadow maps).

    Atlases and mipmaps

    Two mip problems are specific to foliage atlases. Neighbouring tiles bleed into each other in small mips unless they are padded, and alpha-tested leaves thin out in the distance as the mips average their alpha:

    Explained once, used here

    Padding: at mip k a texel covers 2^k texels, so padding p keeps tiles clean down to mip ⌊log₂ p⌋.

    Open the full explanation
    Explained once, used here

    Coverage: rescale the alpha of each mip so the share of texels above the cutoff stays the same (Unity: Mip Maps Preserve Coverage).

    Open the full explanation

    Texture arrays avoid the bleeding: each slice has its own mip chain. For atlases, pad generously (8–16 texels) and fill the padding with dilated leaf colour, so pixels near the cutout edge never pick up the background colour.

    Build it in Unity HDRP

    The route sets up alpha-tested foliage in HDRP, measures its overdraw with the Rendering Debugger, moves shadow casting to a cheaper proxy, and builds a texture array for grass and small plants. Written for Unity 6 with HDRP 17; Unity 2022 LTS works the same apart from a few renamed menu entries.

    Shader GraphFoliage Lit graph: Alpha Clipping, a separate shadow clip threshold.
    Engine settingsFrame Settings: depth prepass; Rendering Debugger: Quad Overdraw.
    Editor scriptTextureArrayBuilder.cs: one Texture2DArray from same-size atlases.
    1. In the foliage graph enable Alpha Clipping and keep Surface Type Opaque. Enable Use Shadow Threshold and set a slightly higher threshold for shadows: shadow maps are low resolution, and thin alpha there only adds cost. Import the leaf atlas with Mip Maps Preserve Coverage and the material's cutoff as Alpha Cutoff Value.
    2. Depth prepass: in HDRP Global Settings > Frame Settings (Camera), Deferred mode renders alpha-clipped opaque objects in the depth prepass when Depth Prepass within Deferred is on; Forward mode always renders a depth prepass. Compare GPU time with it on and off on your worst foliage view.
    3. Measure: Window > Analysis > Rendering Debugger > Rendering > Fullscreen Debug Mode = Quad Overdraw. Hot areas over crowns show where cutouts or fewer cards pay off. Check the same view in the GPU profiler or RenderDoc for the foliage passes' time.
    4. Shadow proxy: give a tree a second, much simpler mesh (merged cluster cards or a few branch quads) with Cast Shadows = Shadows Only, and set Cast Shadows = Off on the visible LODs. For grass, turn shadow casting off entirely.
    5. Texture array: put TextureArrayBuilder.cs in Assets/Editor, select the grass atlases (same size, format and mip count) and run Assets > Create > Foliage Texture Array. In the graph, add a Texture 2D Array property and a Sample Texture 2D Array node; feed the slice index from vertex colour, a UV channel or a per-instance property.
    // Assets/Editor/TextureArrayBuilder.cs: select leaf atlases of the same size and format,
// then Assets > Create > Foliage Texture Array. Slice order = selection order sorted by name.
using System.Linq;
using UnityEditor;
using UnityEngine;
using UnityEngine.Experimental.Rendering;   // GraphicsFormatUtility

public static class TextureArrayBuilder
{
    [MenuItem("Assets/Create/Foliage Texture Array")]
    static void Build()
    {
        var tex = Selection.objects.OfType<Texture2D>().OrderBy(t => t.name).ToArray();
        if (tex.Length == 0) { Debug.LogError("Select the textures first."); return; }
        var first = tex[0];
        foreach (var t in tex)
            if (t.width != first.width || t.height != first.height || t.format != first.format || t.mipmapCount != first.mipmapCount)
            { Debug.LogError($"{t.name}: size, format and mip count must match {first.name}."); return; }

        var arr = new Texture2DArray(first.width, first.height, tex.Length, first.format, first.mipmapCount > 1,
                                     !GraphicsFormatUtility.IsSRGBFormat(first.graphicsFormat))
        { wrapMode = first.wrapMode, filterMode = first.filterMode, anisoLevel = first.anisoLevel };
        for (int s = 0; s < tex.Length; s++)
            for (int m = 0; m < first.mipmapCount; m++)
                Graphics.CopyTexture(tex[s], 0, m, arr, s, m);   // copies compressed data as is: no re-encode

        string path = AssetDatabase.GetAssetPath(first).Replace(first.name, "LeafArray");
        path = System.IO.Path.ChangeExtension(path, ".asset");
        AssetDatabase.CreateAsset(arr, path);
        Debug.Log($"Texture array with {tex.Length} slices: {path}\n" + string.Join("\n", tex.Select((t, i) => $"  {i}: {t.name}")));
    }
}

    The builder copies compressed mip data unchanged, so the array costs exactly what the separate textures cost; it saves materials and batches, not memory.

    URP: Forward+ renders a depth prepass when a feature needs it (or set Depth Priming); the rest is identical.

    Build it in Unreal Engine

    Unreal's foliage is masked opaque by default. The settings that matter are the early depth pass, the overdraw view modes, shadow settings per foliage type, and texture arrays for many small species. Written for Unreal Engine 5.6 and later. Substrate is production-ready from 5.7; check Project Settings > Rendering > Substrate to see which material model a project uses.

    MaterialMasked foliage material with a tuned Opacity Mask Clip Value.
    Project SettingsProject Settings: Early Z-pass for masked meshes.
    AssetTexture 2D Array for grass and small plants.
    1. Material: Blend Mode Masked, Two Sided, Opacity Mask Clip Value around 0.33–0.5. In the texture, enable Preserve Alpha Coverage with the same value (Unreal names it Alpha Coverage Thresholds in recent versions) so distant foliage keeps its density.
    2. Project Settings > Rendering > Optimizations > Early Z-pass: Opaque and masked meshes renders masked foliage into the depth prepass, so the base pass shades each pixel once. Compare GPU time with stat gpu or Unreal Insights.
    3. Overdraw: the viewport's Optimization Viewmodes > Quad Overdraw and Shader Complexity show where cards stack. Tighter card outlines from SpeedTree or your card generator reduce both.
    4. Shadows per foliage type or HISM component: turn off Cast Shadow for grass, use Cast Dynamic Shadow only where needed, and rely on Virtual Shadow Map caching for slowly moving foliage. A low-detail shadow-only mesh works as in Unity (a second component with Hidden Shadow).
    5. Texture arrays: create a Texture 2D Array asset from same-size textures, sample it with Texture Sample on a Texture2DArray parameter and a slice index from PerInstanceRandom, vertex colour or a UV channel.

    With Nanite, masked materials need programmable rasterisation and cost more than opaque Nanite geometry; Epic's guidance for Nanite foliage is modelled, opaque leaves where the triangle budget allows.
