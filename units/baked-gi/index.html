    Baked Light: Lightmaps and Probes

    Most of the light indoors has bounced at least once. This unit explains indirect light, then the ways games store it: lightmaps, spherical harmonics probes and reflection probes, and where each one breaks.

    Builds on the light units and the slab test. Engine notes cover Unity HDRP, Unity URP and Unreal.

    Light that bounces

    A room lit through one window is not black outside the sunbeam. The sunlit patch on the floor reflects light onto the walls, the walls onto the ceiling, and so on. That is often most of what you see indoors, and it carries colour: a red carpet tints the walls red. Global illumination (GI) is the name for computing it.

    One bounce follows from formulas you know. A sunlit wall reflects ρ·E (lux to nits); a surface facing it receives that light weighted by how much of its view the wall fills, the :

    Each further bounce multiplies by ρ·F again, so with typical albedos of 0.3 to 0.6 the light fades within a few bounces. Computing this for every surface point toward every other is far too slow per frame, so most games compute it ahead of time and store the result: that is baking.

    Lightmaps

    A is a texture laid over the static geometry that stores the light arriving at each point, baked offline by a path tracer. Every surface needs its own space in it, so lightmaps use a second, non-overlapping UV set (UV1 in Unity, often called the lightmap UV), with padding between islands so filtering does not bleed light across them (filtering).     Lightmaps are cheap at runtime (one texture sample) and capture soft, multi-bounce light and contact shadows that real-time methods struggle with. In return they are static: moving a light or opening a door means baking again, and dynamic objects need another method to receive the same light: probes.

    Light from a whole sky

    A point outdoors is lit by the entire sky, not one direction. The light it receives, its , adds up every direction above the surface weighted by the cosine (Lambert).     Spherical harmonics

    Real environments are not two flat halves. Storing irradiance for every possible normal direction would need a whole cubemap per point. But irradiance is so smooth that a handful of numbers describe it well. (SH) are to directions what a Fourier series is to a waveform: band 0 is the average, band 1 a single "brighter on one side" gradient, band 2 adds finer variation. Three bands are 9 coefficients per colour channel.

    Below is a slice of the idea for light that only varies with height (a bright sky window around the zenith): the light, its approximation with the first bands, and the irradiance a surface receives at each tilt:

    The light itself is approximated badly: a sharp window cannot be built from three smooth bands. The irradiance, however, is almost exact, because the cosine weighting already blurs away everything the higher bands would carry. That is why light probes store L2 SH (9 coefficients) and why it works for diffuse light only: shiny reflections need the detail, so they get reflection probes.

    Light probes and leaking

    A is a point where the baker stores the incoming light as SH. Dynamic objects (characters, props) and small static ones read the probes around them and interpolate between them. Interpolation does not know about walls, though. A probe outside in the sun and a probe inside a dark room blend straight through the wall between them:

    That is : dark rooms with glowing characters, or sunlit balconies that look dull. Fixes: put probes on both sides of every wall close to it, and use systems that know about geometry. Adaptive probe volumes and DDGI store visibility or validity per probe and ignore probes behind a surface.

    Reflection probes and box projection

    Shiny surfaces need sharp environment light. A captures a cubemap from one point, prefiltered by roughness (light from the environment). Looking it up with the reflection direction alone treats the room as infinitely far away, so reflections slide over the floor as you walk. Box projection fixes that for rooms: intersect the reflection ray with the room's box (the slab test), then look up the direction from the probe's centre to that hit:

    The correction is exact for points on the box walls and approximate for furniture inside. Outdoors there is no box, so reflection probes there are left unprojected.

    Baked, probe-based and real-time GI

    Today's engines mix the approaches. The choice is a trade between quality, memory, iteration time and how much may move:

    MethodMoving lightsMoving objectsCostTypical use
    Lightmapsnoreceive via probesmemory, bake timestatic levels, mobile
    Probe grids / APVno (or blend bakes)yessmall memory, leaksopen worlds, dynamic props
    Reflection probesno (or re-render)yescubemap memoryshiny surfaces everywhere
    Real-time GI (Lumen, SSGI, DDGI)yesyesGPU time every framehigh-end, changing worlds

    Adaptive Probe Volumes (APV) place probes automatically by geometry density, store validity to reduce leaking, and support blending between baked lighting scenarios (day, night). Lightmaps are baked with the GPU or CPU progressive lightmapper; Screen Space Global Illumination and ray-traced GI add real-time bounces.

    URP supports lightmaps, light probes (classic tetrahedral probe groups) and, in recent versions, Adaptive Probe Volumes as well. Reflection probes support box projection; blending between probes is optional per URP asset.

    Lumen is the default real-time GI and reflection system in UE5: it traces against simplified scene representations every frame, so no baking is needed. Baked lightmaps (Lightmass / GPU Lightmass) remain for platforms without Lumen. Sphere and box Reflection Captures are still used as a fallback.

    Build it in Unity HDRP

    Baked lighting is set up in the Lighting window and on each object; the script makes the probes visible by evaluating their spherical harmonics along a line, the same data shaders use for ambient light. Written for Unity 6 with HDRP 17; Unity 2022 LTS works the same apart from a few renamed menu entries.

    Engine settingsLighting window, static flags, lightmap UVs, probe system, reflection probes.
    C# scriptProbeSampler.cs: draws the irradiance light probes give along a line.
    1. Mark scenery that does not move as Static (at least Contribute GI). On its Mesh Renderer, Receive Global Illumination chooses Lightmaps (unique UV2 required, see model import) or Light Probes (small or detailed objects).
    2. Window > Rendering > Lighting: Lightmapper Progressive GPU, Lightmap Resolution in texels per unit (the density of lightmaps), Lightmap Padding, Directional Mode. Bake with Generate Lighting.
    3. Lights: Mode Baked (only in lightmaps), Mixed (baked indirect, real-time direct) or Realtime.
    4. Probes: in the HDRP asset set Light Probe System to Adaptive Probe Volumes and add an Adaptive Probe Volume covering the level; bake in the Lighting window's Adaptive Probe Volumes tab. Its leak reduction and virtual offset options handle probe leaking. Classic Light Probe Groups work when Light Probe System is set to Light Probe Groups.
    5. Reflections: GameObject > Light > Reflection Probe (HDRP version). Fit the Influence Volume box to the room; its box shape gives the box projection from box projection. Bake, or set it to real-time for moving scenes.
    6. Add ProbeSampler.cs to an empty GameObject and walk it through a doorway: the gizmo spheres show the ambient colour light probes give at each point (Light Probe Groups only).
    using UnityEngine;
using UnityEngine.Rendering;

// Shows the irradiance the baked light probes give at points along this object's forward axis.
// Works with Light Probe Groups (not Adaptive Probe Volumes).
public class ProbeSampler : MonoBehaviour
{
    public int samples = 12;
    public float length = 6f;
    public Vector3 normal = Vector3.up;      // direction of the surface being lit

    readonly Vector3[] dirs = new Vector3[1];
    readonly Color[] result = new Color[1];

    void OnDrawGizmos()
    {
        dirs[0] = normal.normalized;
        for (int i = 0; i < samples; i++)
        {
            Vector3 p = transform.position + transform.forward * (length * i / Mathf.Max(samples - 1, 1));
            LightProbes.GetInterpolatedProbe(p, null, out SphericalHarmonicsL2 sh);   // 9 coefficients per channel
            sh.Evaluate(dirs, result);
            Color c = result[0];
            float m = Mathf.Max(c.maxColorComponent, 1e-4f);
            Gizmos.color = m > 1f ? c / m : c;                // keep the hue when it is brighter than 1
            Gizmos.DrawSphere(p, 0.15f);
        }
    }
}

    A sudden change in colour between two spheres at a wall is the leak the unit describes: the interpolation sees probes on both sides. Add probes along walls and doorways, or rely on APV's validity and leak reduction.

    Same Lighting window, static flags and lightmapper. URP supports Adaptive Probe Volumes in Unity 6 (URP asset > Lighting > Light Probe System) and box-projected reflection probes (Box Projection on the probe, enabled in the URP asset).

    Baked lighting: Lightmass (or GPU Lightmass) with static lights and a lightmap UV channel; Volumetric Lightmaps act as probes for movable objects. Unreal 5's default is Lumen real-time GI; Sphere and Box Reflection Captures for baked reflections.
